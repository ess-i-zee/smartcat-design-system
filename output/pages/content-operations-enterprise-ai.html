Why content operations are the next frontier in enterprise AI

Benchmark data from 200+ enterprise leaders shows why connected content workflows are the next frontier for global growth, and how to get there.

There are two ways to become a global company. One is presence: being in markets with inconsistent, patched-together content that feels chaotic and expensive to run. The other is operation: maintaining a living content system with coordinated workflows that create a first-mover advantage. That gap between having a global presence and running a global content operation is the strategic divide enterprise leaders should be focused on in 2026.

The complexity drivers differ by function. L&D teams cite regulatory and compliance velocity as their top pressure (50%); marketing teams point to channel expansion (51%) and brand integrity and safety (50%). Both functions felt it sharply: 75% of L&D teams and 71% of marketing teams saw at least a 25% year-over-year jump in total content production workload.

80%

Report accelerated content creation with AI

68%

64%

None of the time saved in step one matters if the content piles up in step eight. No respondents reported a fully autonomous, end-to-end content workflow, and 26% of enterprise teams say their content workflows are still entirely human-driven with no AI integration at all.

The missing layer: orchestration

67% of teams have only partially integrated content tech stacks. Just 12% report a unified, fully orchestrated stack. The bottleneck is not model capability but workflow connectivity — when stacks are fragmented, even small changes create rework across languages and formats because teams cannot route work through one shared workflow.

What the highest-ROI teams do differently

Platform consolidation

Unified AI tech stacks are 1.6x more likely to report the highest ROI than fragmented ones.

Deeper automation

Process-level automation, not just task-level, is 1.7x more likely to achieve the highest ROI.

Speed to market

Highest-ROI teams are 6.5x more likely to report 50%+ faster localization and globalization workflows.

Less review friction

30% more likely to report no or minimal governance and compliance review delays.

At scale, the bottleneck shifts from model capability to approvals: 38% of enterprises say security, legal, or compliance reviews often or always delay AI platform rollouts.

The training gap underneath all of it

58% of enterprises rely on self-serve AI learning (34%) or no formal training at all (24%). Teams with structured training are 2x more likely to reach process-level automation and 1.4x more likely to see 50%+ faster localization than teams with informal or no training. Life sciences formalizes AI training fastest, driven by heavy regulatory-velocity pressure.
