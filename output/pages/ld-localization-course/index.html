A working method, not a theory lesson

Most localization advice is written for marketing copy. Learning content behaves differently: it is modular, versioned constantly, wrapped in proprietary file formats, and — when it is compliance training — legally load-bearing in every market it ships to. This course covers what changes because of that, and what to do about it.

Frequently asked questions

What counts as L&D content?

Anything an organization publishes to teach its own people: onboarding paths, compliance and safety training, product and sales enablement, technical certification, leadership programs — and the learning platform those courses live in, which is part of the experience whether or not anyone budgeted for it.

Do we have to localize everything?

No, and trying to is the most common way these programs stall. Compliance and safety training usually has to reach learners in a language they actually work in. Enablement and leadership content is a judgment call. A large tail of most mature catalogs is better retired than translated. Module 3 covers how to make that call deliberately rather than by whoever asks loudest.

Can AI translate a whole course on its own?

It can translate the text, and for low-risk internal reference material that is often enough. Assessments, regulatory content, and anything a learner is certified against still warrant human review — not because the raw output is poor, but because a wrong answer key or a mistranslated safety instruction is expensive in a way a clumsy sentence is not.

What about voiceover and subtitles?

They are separate deliverables on separate clocks, and they are usually where a project runs late. Subtitles come from a timed text file and are quick to produce and update. Voiceover needs a translated and timed script, a recording session, and a re-render of the video. Module 2 covers how to plan and price both.

How do we handle courses that change every quarter?

Treat the first localization as setup, not as the project. Translation memory and a glossary mean the second version only pays for what actually changed. Without them, every update is a fresh full-price translation, which is how a program that looked affordable in year one quietly stops being renewed in year two.

Who is this course for?

L&D managers and instructional designers who are being asked to go multilingual, and localization or content-operations leads who have just inherited a training catalog. No prior localization experience is assumed.
