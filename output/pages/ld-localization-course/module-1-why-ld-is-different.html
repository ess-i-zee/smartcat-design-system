Why L&D content is different

Module 1 of 3 · About 15 minutes. Learning content is assessed, packaged, and in some markets legally required. Each of those three facts breaks an assumption the standard localization playbook makes about text.

Course overview

Three things a course does that a web page doesn't

It is assessed, not just read

A landing page succeeds if someone clicks. A course succeeds if someone can do something afterwards, and you find out with a quiz. That makes assessment items the highest-risk text in the whole package: a distractor that turns ambiguous in translation, or an answer key that no longer lines up with its option, quietly converts a pass rate into noise. Translate a question, its options, its feedback, and its key as one unit — never as separate strings — and have someone who knows the subject confirm that exactly one answer is still correct.

It ships as a package, not a page

Most courses leave their authoring tool as a SCORM or xAPI package — a zip holding HTML, a manifest, media files, and tracking logic. The translatable text is scattered across several of those files, and some of it is not text at all: it has been rendered into exported images and burned into video frames. Pulling it out by hand is how teams discover, three weeks in, that a third of the course cannot be updated without rebuilding it in the tool it came from.

It is legally load-bearing in some markets

Safety, harassment, and financial-conduct training are not simply good practice. In many jurisdictions they have to reach the worker in a language they genuinely understand, and it is the employer who carries the burden of showing that they did. That lifts compliance content out of the nice-to-have-translated column entirely, and it makes the audit trail part of the deliverable: who translated it, who reviewed it, and which version each learner actually completed.

What to do with that

Split by legal exposure first

Sort the catalog by what is legally required before you sort it by language. The two lists rarely match.

Treat a quiz as one unit

Question, options, feedback, and answer key travel together, and a subject-matter expert signs off on each.

Ask for the source project

The authoring file, not the published package. It is the only version you can actually re-export.

Find the text inside images

On-screen labels rendered into graphics are the most common source of surprise cost on a course.

Plan for version two

Courses are updated, not finished. Set up translation memory and a glossary on the very first pass.

Keep the audit trail

Record who translated, who reviewed, and which version of the course each learner completed.
