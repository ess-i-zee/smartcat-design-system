Know your formats before you quote

Module 2 of 3 · About 15 minutes. A course's words live in three separate layers, and only one of them behaves like a normal document. Here is where to look, and what each layer costs you in time.

Course overview

Where the words actually are

The authoring layer

Articulate Rise and Storyline, Adobe Captivate, iSpring, and Lectora all export a translation file — usually XLIFF, sometimes a structured Word document — and all of them import that file back into the project. This is the path you want: text comes out, gets translated, goes back in, and the course republishes with its layout, branching, and interactions intact. It only works if someone can hand you the source project, which is why "just send us the SCORM zip" is the wrong first request to make.

The media layer

Video and audio are separate deliverables on separate clocks. Subtitles come from a timed text file, so they are fast to produce, cheap to fix, and easy to re-cut when the source changes. Voiceover needs a translated script timed to the original, a recording session, and a re-render of the finished video — and any words that appear in the footage itself need the source project to change at all. Decide subtitles versus voiceover per course rather than per programme: a two-minute safety briefing and a forty-minute certification module do not deserve the same treatment.

The platform layer

Underneath the courses sits the learning platform itself: course titles and descriptions, learning-path names, category labels, enrolment emails, certificates, and the interface a learner navigates to reach any of it. Translating every course and leaving this layer in English is a common and very visible miss, because it is the part the learner meets first. It is usually a modest string file rather than a real project, which makes it the cheapest win available and an odd one to skip.

A scoping checklist

Ask for source projects

The authoring file itself, not the published output. Confirm someone can still open it.

Count the media minutes

Keep subtitle minutes and voiceover minutes on separate lines. They price and schedule differently.

Flag baked-in text

List every graphic and video frame with words rendered into it. Each one needs the source file.

Inventory the assessments

Question count, question types, and who is able to sign off that the answers still hold.

Include the platform strings

Titles, descriptions, learning paths, certificates, and the enrolment emails learners receive.

Leave room to expand

German and Russian often run longer than English. Fixed-width buttons and tight slides break first.
