Build a workflow that survives version two

Module 3 of 3 · About 15 minutes. Almost every L&D localization programme can translate one course. The ones that last are the ones set up so the second version costs a fraction of the first.

Course overview

From project to process

Tier the catalog by risk

Start by sorting, not by translating. Compliance and safety training goes in the first tier: required in the learner's working language, and reviewed by a person every time. Enablement, product, and onboarding content goes in the second: high value, moderate risk, usually AI translation with a human pass. Everything else goes in a third tier you should be honest about — much of a mature catalog is stale, duplicated, or barely completed, and translating it is the most expensive way to avoid retiring it.

Set up the linguistic assets first

A glossary, a translation memory, and a short style guide are the difference between paying once and paying every quarter. The glossary locks product names, role titles, system labels, and regulatory terms so they stop drifting between courses. The memory means the next version of a course only costs what genuinely changed. The style guide settles tone and formality per market — whether your training addresses a learner formally is a real decision in German, Japanese, and Korean, and it is far easier made once than argued course by course.

Connect it, then measure it

A workflow that depends on someone remembering to export is a workflow that stops. Connect the authoring tool and the learning platform so a new source version triggers its own translation job, and route review to the in-market people who already know the content. Then measure what you actually wanted: completion and pass rates by locale, time from source publish to localized launch, and cost per learning hour. Word counts and turnaround times measure the vendor; those three measure the programme.

The launch checklist

Tiers agreed and written down

Someone outside L&D can tell which courses must be localized, and say why.

Glossary approved per language

Reviewed by an in-market person before the first course ships, not after the first complaint.

Translation memory connected

So the second version of a course prices as an update rather than as a new project.

Reviewers named per market

A specific person, not a team inbox, for each language and each risk tier.

Pipeline triggered automatically

A new source version starts its own job, with no manual export standing in the way.

Baseline metrics captured

Completion, pass rate, and time-to-launch recorded before you change anything.
