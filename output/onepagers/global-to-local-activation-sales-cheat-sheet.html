Global-to-Local Content Activation — Sales Cheat Sheet

Internal only

The pitch

Smartcat automates the work between global content creation and local execution — translating, adapting, validating, reviewing, synchronizing, and delivering content across every market and channel.

Don’t sell translation volume — sell the operational complexity around it: the manual coordination, adaptation, review, rebuilding, and publishing work that happens before, around, and after translation.

Discovery questions that open the door

  • What content is created centrally, and what happens once the global version is approved?

  • Do markets simply translate the source or adapt it? What are they allowed to change?

  • Where are the rules — approved claims, terminology, market requirements — documented? (Excel, PDFs, tribal knowledge = opportunity)

  • How many teams touch content between global approval and local launch? Where does someone manually move it between systems?

  • Who approves local content? What happens after a small edit?

  • Once approved, who puts it into the CMS, e-commerce, CRM, packaging, partner portals?

The three questions that uncover the deal

1 — The gap

“Once the global asset is approved, what still has to happen before every market can actually use it?”

2 — The manual work

“Which parts of that process are still manual?”

3 — The highest-value pain

“What happens when the global source changes?”

Change management is often the highest-value discovery area: manual version comparison, markets missing updates, entire files recreated because one section changed.

Signals to go deeper — if the customer says any of these

“Every market does its own version.”

“A small update creates a huge downstream exercise.”

“We manage it in spreadsheets.”

“The translation is easy; the workflow around it is the hard part.”

“The agency rebuilds everything.”

“We copy content between systems.”

“Legal has to review every version.”

“Each channel needs a different version.”

“We don’t know whether markets have the latest content.”

“We can’t scale this process without adding more people.”

Qualification — a strong opportunity has at least 4 of 6

  • 1

    A central global source exists

  • 2

    Local markets materially adapt the content

  • 3

    Manual handoffs between systems or teams

  • 4

    Governance, compliance, or approvals required

  • 5

    Significant scale (10+ markets/languages, frequent launches)

  • 6

    Content activated across multiple channels

Best-fit accounts: heavy global content creation · frequent campaigns/launches · manual agency production · complex approvals · large CMS/DAM/PIM ecosystems · version-control problems · high local content volume · difficulty managing global changes. Think in terms of products × markets × languages × channels × changes × reviewers.

Build the ROI on five pillars

Production

Reduced agency spend, fewer manual builds, less copy/paste

Operations

Less project management, fewer handoffs, less duplicate work

Speed

Faster market activation, launches, and updates

Risk

Fewer compliance mistakes, better governance & auditability

Capacity

More content supported by the same organization

The sales mindset

Don’t ask ‘How much do you translate?’ — ask ‘How does global content actually become usable in every market?’

Translation is the easiest part of the workflow to see. The bigger opportunity is usually everything around it.
